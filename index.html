Identity & voice

Every thinker you need, rendered, on demand.

Cast is a structured registry of 240+ named thinkers — thought leaders, archetypes, peers — each with real substance behind the name: voice patterns, mental models, stances, tensions. Not a list of labels. A roster you can actually convene, query, and think with.

The problem it solves

A name without substance is just a label. Cast is the substance.

Most systems that claim to use "expert personas" are faking it. They attach a famous name to a generic output and call it a point of view. Cast is different: every entity in the registry carries a corpus — actual mental models, documented stances, captured vocabulary, recorded tensions. When Voices convenes a critique panel and seats Marty Cagan next to Teresa Torres, both voices render from real material, not from a model's approximation of their reputations. The difference is whether the room is populated or merely labeled.

The most valuable expert in the room is the one who knows how to think, not just what they concluded.
How you use it

Find the right voices. Seat them. Let them speak.

1

Find by role, domain, or stance

The registry is taxonomically coded — O*NET roles, NAICS industries, controlled vocabularies — so you can filter for exactly the perspective your problem needs, not whoever happens to be top of mind.

2

Convene the panel

Named humans, composite archetypes, or inner-circle peers — Cast supplies the identity layer. Voices and Forge consume it to run critiques, build personas, and render judgment channels in each voice's actual cadence.

3

The substance travels with them

Every voice carries its corpus into the room: decision patterns, tensions, characteristic vocabulary. The rendering is grounded — each thinker pushes back in the way they actually would, not in the way the model assumes they would.

What makes it different

The governing principle: identity and substance are inseparable.

The rule the whole product is built around

A registry entry without a corpus is a placeholder, not a voice.

Cast enforces a three-layer contract on every entity it holds. Identity is closed-world: structured, enumerated, taxonomically coded — findable and deduplicated. Corpus is open-world: the richer the better, no vocabulary constraints. Rendering is downstream: Cast declares identity, Forge and Voices project from it. The chain is one-way — renderings are projections, the registry is upstream — so you can re-render any voice as the corpus deepens without touching the consumers:

Identity layer

Who the thinker is — closed-world, enumerated fields, controlled vocabularies. Powers findability, deduplication, and cross-referencing across the whole system.

Corpus layer

What the thinker actually thinks — open-world substance. Voice patterns, mental models, stances, tensions. The raw material that makes rendering real rather than performed.
